## Locked clinical technique

1. Patient hand is relaxed and pronated, dorsum up.
2. Examiner isolates the **third digit (middle finger)**.
3. One hand stabilizes/supports the middle finger proximal to the distal interphalangeal joint (DIP), without obscuring the target.
4. The other hand contacts the nail/distal phalanx and produces a **brief palmward DIP flexion followed by immediate release/rebound**.
5. Observe the same hand for **involuntary thumb flexion/adduction and/or index-finger flexion**.
6. Ring/little-finger movement is not required as the defining positive response.

## Interpretation lock

- Hoffmann sign is an upper motor neuron/corticospinal-tract sign.
- It can raise suspicion for degenerative cervical myelopathy in the appropriate clinical context.
- It does **not** diagnose or exclude degenerative cervical myelopathy by itself.
- Interpret with hand dexterity, deep tendon reflexes, other pathologic reflexes, gait, strength/sensation, and cervical MRI when clinically indicated.
